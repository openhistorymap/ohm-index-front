Methodology

How the index is kept

The Data Index considers a small set of research areas that, taken together, give a sense of how completely we know a given period and place. Every record is classified along these areas, sub-topics, and two quality indicators. The categories are deliberately broad; precision lives in the records themselves.

i.

Research areas

  • Agriculture

    Land use, crops cultivated, agrarian practices recovered from cadasters, surveys, and archaeology.

  • Climate

    Reconstructions of past climates from instrumental records, proxies, and historical accounts.

  • Economy

    Trade, prices, wages — and the careful work of mapping ancient values onto modern terms.

  • Entertainment

    Circus, theatre, cinema, festivals: the public life of leisure across periods.

  • Industry

    The industrial fabric of the past: mills, mines, factories, supply chains.

  • Infrastructure — roads

    Road networks, post routes, pilgrim ways, and their evolution.

  • Infrastructure — water

    Canals, ports, navigable rivers, aqueducts, water-borne trade.

  • Infrastructure — air

    Air routes, aerodromes, and the modern era of aviation infrastructure.

  • Politics

    Borders, jurisdictions, and the redrawing of administrative space.

  • Religion

    Distribution of communities, places of worship, pilgrimages, ecclesiastical administration.

  • Urban

    Cities and towns: their footprints, growth, decline, plans, demolitions.

  • Ephemeral

    Movements: of people, ships, armies — anything in motion that leaves a trace.

  • War

    Battles, fortifications, sieges, military geography.

  • Geography

    The shape of the earth itself, both natural and shaped by human hands.

ii.

Sub-topics

Each area carries a second axis: a sub-topic that says what kind of evidence a record is. A point on a map, a building plan, a 3D model, a timeline of events — the same area answers different questions depending on this axis.

  • Location

    Point localization of items across an area.

  • Structure

    Planimetry of buildings, cities, regions.

  • Model

    3D models of buildings or items in the area.

  • Events

    Timelines of activity within the area.

  • Usage

    Context and mode of use — how the thing was actually inhabited.

  • Indexes

    Quantitative indicators (wages, GDP, prices) reconstructed and normalized.

  • General

    Records that cover the area broadly without a single viewpoint.

iii.

Two quality indicators

Beyond classification, every record carries two scores between 1 and 6: source reliability and source precision. They are not absolute and they are not algorithmic — they reflect editorial judgement against a published rubric.

Source reliability

  1. 6Peer-reviewed academic research, excavation reports.
  2. 5In-period source material.
  3. 4Review papers.
  4. 3Non-peer-reviewed research.
  5. 2Local public-history activities.
  6. 1Hearsay.

Source precision

  1. 6Precise dataset with well-defined methodologies and tools.
  2. 5High precision, methodology partially documented.
  3. 4Good precision, methodology inferable.
  4. 3Mixed precision, errors recoverable from context.
  5. 2Visible discrepancies, methodology unclear.
  6. 1Low quality, errors and ambiguities throughout.
iv.

Add to the catalog

If you know of a source or dataset that belongs here, write to index@openhistorymap.org. The catalog grows in public, and additions are reviewed against this methodology.
