A reading room of the past · est. 2017

A library of where, when, and who said it first.

The Open History Map Data Index is a working catalog of the sources, datasets, and regions that the Open History Map project draws on. It exists because finding a usable historical dataset is harder than converting one — you have to know what to ask for, and in whose vocabulary.

Open the index or browse sources, datasets, how it’s made.

i.

Why this exists

Since the project began, the most consistently difficult operation has not been conversion or normalization — those are tractable engineering problems — but search. The right dataset for a question about, say, the parishes of sixteenth-century Bologna lives under whatever vocabulary its compiler chose, often a vocabulary the seeker does not share. The OHM Data Index is a small attempt at the switchboard between the two.

It draws on the spirit of the OKFN Open Data Index, published yearly to track the openness of public data across countries, but turns the same instrument toward sources for the past: who recorded a place, when, in what tradition, with what evidence still standing.

iii.

Add to the catalog

If you know of a source or dataset that belongs here, the contribute page is how to reach the editors. The catalog grows slowly, in public, with each addition reviewed against the methodology.
